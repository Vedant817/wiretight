## (2) The idea we'd back: **"Clearline"** (working name)
**A receivables-reconciliation agent that lives where the money lands: Airwallex Global Accounts.** For every incoming deposit, it matches the money to open invoices, explains any gap, decides what to do about it (close, reclassify, chase, refund, or hold), and then puts the confirmed cash to work through FX. Each decision is gated by policy written in code.

**Why it wins, criterion by criterion**
- **Real operator problem.** All three cited pains point at the same job, and AR teams do it every day.
- **The decision changes with evidence.** Example: a $9,700 deposit against a $10,000 invoice starts out as "chase the $300". Then a remittance PDF arrives saying 3% tax was withheld, and the plan becomes "close the invoice, book a WHT receivable, request the certificate". This is exactly the guide's beat: goal → plan → new information → revised decision → resulting state.
- **Moves money with a human in the loop.** Refund transfers and FX conversions require approval. Updating invoice state and posting small variances are autonomous within policy limits.
- **Holds up across markets and currencies.** It runs on multi-currency Global Accounts in USD, EUR, and GBP. Variance patterns are per corridor (for example, the guide's flat EUR 12.85 SWIFT fee), and withholding tax is handled by jurisdiction.
- **Credible adoption path.** Airwallex customers already receive money into Global Accounts, so reconciliation is the obvious next job. It's native to the account, with no bank-feed lag, and plugs into the accounting partner. (The partner listed as "AI accounting, MCP server, sandbox" appears to be Light; this is inferred from the logo file and needs confirming.)
- **Differentiation.** The guide calls this territory unclaimed. Most of the 433+ registrants will cluster on Kits 1–10. That matters for Founder's and Judges' Choice. Clearline does not target the Visa or Metal awards.

**Airwallex capabilities used:** `GET /global_accounts`, `GET /deposits` and `/global_accounts/{id}/transactions` (payer name, bank, and reference), `GET /balances/current`, `POST /simulation/deposit/create` (supports `payer_name`, `payer_country`, `reference`, and `status`), `/beneficiaries/validate`, `/beneficiaries/create`, `/transfers/create` and the transfer simulations (refunds), `/fx/rates/current`, `/fx/quotes/create`, `/fx/conversions/create` (REST only), webhooks (`balance.ga.top_up`), and both MCP servers (docs and developer).

**Guardrails that make it trustworthy**
- **Policy as code, not prompts.** Auto-close only if the variance is at or under a per-currency cap and matches a known fee or FX pattern. Everything else becomes a proposal. The model reads remittance text and explains; code does all the arithmetic.
- **Approval bound to exact terms.** Every refund, conversion over a threshold, and customer email shows an approval card with amount, currency, counterparty, and an evidence hash. If any of those change, the agent asks again.
- **Refund only to the source.** Refunds go back to the originating payer account taken from the deposit record. The agent never pays bank details it found in an email, which blocks BEC-style redirection. A refund is capped at the overpaid amount, with one refund per deposit.
- **Idempotency.** Each operation gets its own `request_id`, plus a duplicate lock. Any ambiguous response triggers a check of the transfer list before a retry.
- **Holds and escalation.** Deposits from unknown payers or amounts that match no invoice are held and escalated, never auto-applied or auto-refunded.
- **Audit trail.** Every decision logs its evidence, the rule that fired, the model's rationale, the approver, and the API result. That log exports as the reconciliation report.
- **Typed tools only.** The model gets typed tools and no raw credentials. Sandbox simulations sit behind one interface.

## (3) Core user flow (5 steps)
1. **Set policy and connect.** The controller connects Global Accounts and an invoice source (synthetic AR ledger or the accounting partner), then sets tolerances, approval thresholds, and per-currency cash buffers.
2. **A deposit lands.** The agent pulls amount, currency, payer, and reference, then proposes a match: exact, one-to-many, or fuzzy. The model reads the reference text, and code checks that the sums tie out.
3. **Explain and decide.** Code computes the variance. The agent classifies the cause (fee, FX, WHT, partial, overpayment, unknown), cites its evidence, and picks an action per policy: auto-close, reclassify, chase, refund, or hold.
4. **Approve and act.** For anything that moves money or contacts a customer, the controller sees an approval card and taps approve. The agent then executes the refund transfer, updates invoice state, and queues the chase draft.
5. **Put the cash to work.** Now that applied cash is known, the agent keeps enough in each currency for upcoming same-currency payables. It converts the surplus via quote then conversion, and posts the audit log and the reconciliation report.

**The demo moment that makes judges get it.** The screen is split. On the left are 6 open invoices across USD, EUR, and GBP. On the right is a live deposit feed.
- A deposit of $9,700 arrives against a $10,000 invoice. A red card appears: "Shortfall $300. Proposed: chase customer," with a drafted email awaiting approval.
- A remittance PDF arrives: "less 3% withholding tax, certificate to follow." The card visibly flips to amber: "Revised: close invoice, book $300 WHT receivable, request certificate. Chase cancelled." The diff between the old plan and the new one is highlighted, with the evidence line quoted.
- A duplicate payment then gets an approval-bound refund to the original payer. A second click on approve is blocked by the duplicate lock.
- The surplus EUR is converted to USD. The balance and reserve update live.
- One sentence for judges: *"It changed its mind for the right reason, showed why, and couldn't pay twice."*

## Risks and open questions (for the board to pressure-test)
- **CEO.** Is reconciliation exciting enough on stage next to shopping agents? Our bet is that the evidence-driven reversal plus the money-out beat carries it. Decide the name and pitch line. Confirm what the Oct 23 idea entry must contain; it isn't published, so check after registering.
- **Staff Engineer.** Check which deposit payer and reference fields the sandbox actually returns. Work out how to build a refund beneficiary from deposit payer details, given the routing-code validation. The deposit webhook (`ga.new`) only exists for API versions up to 2025-02-14, so plan to poll `/deposits` or use `balance.ga.top_up`. FX booking is REST only.
- **Banking Domain.** Are our short-pay patterns realistic per corridor (OUR/SHA/BEN charge codes)? What are normal tolerance levels? How does WHT work on cross-border services, especially where India is the payer? What are typical refund mechanics for overpayments?
- **Compliance & Risk.** Set the refund-to-source policy and decide how unknown payers and misdirected funds are held (AML angle). Define the agent's authority boundaries and what customer or remittance data may enter model context. Check that the audit log is sufficient for an auditor.
- **Known gaps.** Judging weights aren't published. There's no public statistic on the share of SWIFT receipts that arrive short. Incumbents (HighRadius, Billtrust) exist, so our differentiator must be clear: native to the account, SME-priced, and able to take actions rather than only suggest matches.
